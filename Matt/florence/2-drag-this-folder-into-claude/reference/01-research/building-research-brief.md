# Building the Research Brief

> **Scope:** How to synthesize data from all four research sources (review mining, keyword analysis, Rufus queries, competitor audit) into a single research brief that drives every visual content decision. This is the bridge between research and execution.
>
> **Related files:** Depends on data from review-mining.md, keyword-analysis.md, rufus-ai-queries.md, and competitor-audit.md. The brief's outputs drive all decisions in /02-visual-content/.

---

## Why the Research Brief Matters

The research brief is the foundation for every visual content decision that follows. No image should be created without a clear link back to something in this brief. It eliminates guesswork and ensures every image slot, every A+ segment, and every creative decision is data-driven.

Without a brief, you're guessing. With a brief, you're executing against customer data.

---

## What the Brief Answers

After gathering data from all four research sources, synthesize it into a brief that answers these five questions:

### 1. Who Is the Customer?

Demographics, context, lifestyle — informed by photo reviews and keyword long-tails.

- Age, gender, lifestyle of the primary buyer
- Where and how they use the product
- What context they live in (urban/suburban, active/sedentary, professional/casual)
- This drives demographic accuracy in all lifestyle photography

### 2. Why Are They Buying?

Primary purchase drivers — informed by review mining and keyword intent.

- The top 3-5 reasons customers buy this product
- Ranked by frequency in reviews and search volume
- These are the benefits that MUST be shown visually in slots 2-3

### 3. What Are They Worried About?

Objections, concerns, unanswered questions — informed by negative reviews + Rufus queries. _(SellerApp Q&A endpoint deprecated in v0.1.5; if a future first-party Q&A source comes online it can be re-added here.)_

- The top 3-5 concerns or questions that slow down the purchase decision
- Each one needs a visual answer — either a listing image or an A+ segment
- These typically drive slots 4-7 and A+ bottom half content

### 4. What's the Competitive Landscape?

Table stakes, gaps, and differentiation opportunities — informed by competitor audit.

- What every competitor shows (table stakes you must match)
- What no competitor shows but customers care about (differentiation opportunity)
- Where the quality benchmark sits and how your content compares
- Main image differentiation opportunities

### 5. What Benefits Must Be Shown Visually?

The final prioritized list of benefits that the images must demonstrate, ranked by frequency in reviews and search volume.

- Top 3-5 benefits for listing images (slots 2-3 get the strongest)
- Supporting content for slots 4-7 (questions, specs, comparisons)
- A+ top half content (emotional, benefit-driven)
- A+ bottom half content (informational, objection-handling)

## From Brief to Image Stack

The research brief directly maps to visual content decisions:

### Main Image Direction
- Competitive differentiation angle (from competitor audit)
- Packaging and presentation approach
- The "scroll stop" strategy for this specific product and category

### Listing Image Stack (Slots 2-7)

Based on the brief's priority ranking:

1. **Slots 2-3:** Highest-priority benefits that must be shown photographically (from purchase drivers + high-volume keywords)
2. **Slots 4-6:** Supporting content — questions to answer, specs to show, comparisons to make (from objections + Rufus queries + table stakes)
3. **Slot 7:** Strong close — last touchpoint before buy decision (value summary, what's included, satisfaction reinforcement)

### A+ Content Structure
- **Top 3 segments:** Reinforce the primary benefits from the brief — photography-forward, emotionally compelling
- **Bottom segments:** Answer the questions and objections from the brief — visual FAQ, details, reassurance

## Brief Format

A complete research brief should include:

```
## Customer Profile
- Primary demographic: [from photo reviews + keyword long-tails]
- Usage context: [where/how they use the product]
- Purchase motivation: [primary reason they're buying]

## Top Benefits to Show (Ranked)
1. [Benefit] — [evidence: X% of reviews, Y search volume]
2. [Benefit] — [evidence]
3. [Benefit] — [evidence]
4. [Benefit] — [evidence]
5. [Benefit] — [evidence]

## Questions/Objections to Address (Ranked)
1. [Question/Objection] — [evidence: review frequency, Rufus queries]
2. [Question/Objection] — [evidence]
3. [Question/Objection] — [evidence]

## Competitive Position
- Table stakes to match: [list]
- Differentiation opportunities: [list]
- Main image differentiation: [specific angle/approach]
- Quality benchmark: [above/at/below]

## Image Stack Recommendation
- Slot 2: [content] — [justification from brief]
- Slot 3: [content] — [justification from brief]
- Slot 4: [content] — [justification from brief]
- Slot 5: [content] — [justification from brief]
- Slot 6: [content] — [justification from brief]
- Slot 7: [content] — [justification from brief]

## A+ Content Direction
- Top half emphasis: [primary benefits to reinforce]
- Bottom half emphasis: [questions/objections to address]
```

---

## The Non-Negotiable Rule

**No image should be created without a clear link back to the research brief.** If you can't point to a specific line in the brief that justifies an image, that image shouldn't exist.

The research drives the content. The content does not come from a formula or a template.
