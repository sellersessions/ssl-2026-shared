01

What is a LoRA

  • LoRA = Low-Rank Adaptation

    A research technique published by Microsoft in 2021 to solve a very specific problem: fine-tuning giant AI models cost millions of dollars. LoRA cut that cost by 10,000× by only retraining a tiny, efficient slice of the model's parameters — called a "low-rank" matrix. This unlocked custom AI for everyone, not just the labs.

    • Low-Rank = math-speak for "a small, efficient shortcut through the model's weight space"
    • Adaptation = you're steering an existing model, not building one from scratch
    • Analogy: the model is the factory. The LoRA is the custom mould you snap in

  • LoRA is a technique, not a tool — it works on any neural network

    Wherever there's a neural network, LoRA can steer it. Text models, image generators, audio models, video models. Same principle every time: take a huge pre-trained model, add a small adapter layer trained on your data.

    • Text (LLMs): brand voice, category knowledge, buyer psychology, product expertise
    • Images (FLUX / Stable Diffusion): visual style, product look, brand aesthetic — consistent imagery at scale
    • Audio / Video: same concept applied to voice, music, and motion

02

Why it matters

  • AI isn't actually "smart" — it's a very sophisticated pattern-matcher

    At its core, a language model is predicting the most statistically probable next word given everything before it. It's not reasoning from first principles. It's combining probabilities derived from everything it was trained on — which is mostly the internet: generic, averaged, not specific to your brand, your customer, or your category. This is why it feels impressive in demos and mediocre in production. The output is calibrated to the average of all input, not the specific reality of your business.

    • This is also why prompting alone has a ceiling — you're still working with a generic base
    • LoRA is how you move past that ceiling by changing what the model is actually trained on

  • Rufus prompts conversations — but that's not the same as real objections

    If you go to a product detail page on Amazon, Rufus has already generated a list of suggested questions — "Is this good for sensitive skin?", "How does this compare to X brand?", "Does it contain artificial flavors?" Those look like customer objections. But they're not. They're conversation starters — statistically probable questions for your category that Rufus surfaces to get buyers clicking and talking. That's actually a good thing for Amazon: more Rufus interactions means more engaged shoppers, which means more likely to close the deal. Rufus is doing its job. The problem is when sellers treat those as the real objection list and build their secondary images around them. They're not your customers' actual hesitations — they're the category's most common conversation topics.

    • Rufus questions = designed to drive engagement, not surface your specific buyer's blockers
    • They're baseline data — useful as a starting point, but they don't know your customer
    • With ProductPinion: ask 100 real shoppers "what would stop you from buying this product?" — then run a second poll to rank those answers by importance. Now you have an actual priority stack of the real objections, in your buyers' own words
    • That ranked list is something Rufus can never give you — and it's also the exact data format you'd use to train an AI on your buyers' psychology
    Key contrast: Rufus gives you the category's conversation starters. A ProductPinion poll gives you your buyers' actual ranked blockers. Only the second one is trainable signal — and only the second one tells you what your secondary images should actually say.

  • The moat isn't the model — it's the data

    GPT-4, Claude, and Gemini are all available to every one of your competitors for the same price. Prompting skill is table stakes in 12 months. The durable advantage is a proprietary dataset that no one else has — your customers' words, their objections, what they trust, what they fear — and a model trained on it. That model works for you specifically. It can't be copied.

04

Every poll is a training data point

  • This is literally how ChatGPT became good — and you can do the same thing

    When OpenAI trained ChatGPT from GPT-3, the biggest unlock wasn't more data — it was human preference feedback. They showed raters two responses and asked "which is better?" Those comparisons, fed back into training, is what made the model actually useful. Sam Altman explained this directly on the Lex Fridman podcast:

    Sam Altman, Lex Fridman Podcast: "The simplest version of this is show two outputs, ask which one is better than the other, which one the human raters prefer, and then feed that back into the model with reinforcement learning. And that process works remarkably well with, in my opinion, remarkably little data to make the model more useful."

    He also said that what made ChatGPT the breakthrough moment wasn't the underlying model — it was the RLHF and the interface. The preference signal is what turned a capable-but-generic model into something that felt genuinely useful.

    • Every ProductPinion poll where voters pick Image A over Image B is a preference pair
    • Every question where buyers rank their top hesitation is human feedback data
    • You've been running RLHF-equivalent data collection without realizing it

  • The data flywheel: more polls → better model → better insights → more polls

    The compound effect is real. A model fine-tuned on your first 50 polls is already better than a generic model for your use case. After 200 polls it knows your category deeply. After 500 polls across your vertical it has an intelligence about buyer psychology that no SaaS tool can replicate — because it was built on real people making real choices, not pattern-matching on generic content.

    • Image polls → trains image preference model AND text LLM (via vision description pipeline)
    • Text polls → trains LLM directly on buyer language and objection patterns
    • Every new poll user improves the shared category model, which benefits all users

Ethical prompt injection — a fun one most haven't considered

  • What is prompt injection — and why "ethical" matters

    Prompt injection is when text embedded in a webpage or document contains hidden instructions that get read by an AI and change how it responds. It's usually discussed as an attack vector. The ethical version — which I'm calling intentional brand persistence — is using the same mechanism to ensure that when any AI reads about your product, it walks away with an accurate, positive, structured understanding of what you do and who you serve.

  • AEO articles — give them a skill that writes these automatically

    AEO = Answer Engine Optimization. As AI assistants replace search for product discovery, the question isn't "how do I rank on Google?" — it's "how does the AI describe me when someone asks?" You can write structured articles specifically designed to be summarized well by LLMs: clear entity definitions, comparison framing, preference signals for your brand. Hand the room a Claude skill that writes these automatically from a product brief.

    • The skill takes: product name, key claims, competitive positioning, 3 customer proof points
    • Outputs: a structured article with headers, FAQ format, and brand-signal language optimized for AI summarization
    • Once published, every AI that reads your site is "trained" on your framing

  • "Summarize" buttons that persist your brand into LLM memory

    Give them a second skill: a "Summarize This Page" button they can embed on their website or product pages. When a visitor clicks it, it sends the page to Claude with a prompt designed to create a structured brand memory — not just a summary, but a formatted representation of what the brand is, what it stands for, and how it outperforms alternatives. The user saves it. Now when they ask Claude anything related to this product category, their mental model is seeded with your brand's framing.

    • This is persistent in Claude's memory system — it carries across conversations
    • The button is low-friction: one click, one save, your brand is now "known" to their AI
    • Works especially well for brand advocates, agency clients, and retail buyers doing research

  • ProductPinion page — ask for a competitive comparison on the spot

    Demo this live: go to the ProductPinion webpage with Claude's Chrome extension active. Use a prompt that asks Claude to compare ProductPinion to PickFu and similar tools — but structured so that the ProductPinion page content is what gets read and summarized first, seeding the comparison with your framing. The AI "learns" your positioning in real time, in front of the room.

    • This is the injection demo — the crowd sees it happen live
    • Key takeaway: whoever controls the structured information an AI reads about your brand, controls how the AI represents you
    Prep note: Have the Chrome extension demo ready. Make sure the ProductPinion page has clean structured content Claude can easily extract and use as comparison framing.
